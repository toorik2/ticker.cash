ticker.cash · referencev25 · chipnet

A notarized BCH/USD price, one transaction a minute.

Thirteen independent notaries each verify a TLS session with one exchange and sign the price they saw. A 373-byte covenant checks seven or more of those signatures, takes the lower median, and writes it on chain together with two Ticker NFTs any transaction can spend to read it. Nobody in the system chooses a number.

§01Overview

The oracle is a single CashTokens category on Bitcoin Cash. Its head is a minting NFT held by the Oracle covenant; its state is a 12-byte commitment: the Unix minute of observation and the price. Every update spends the head and recreates it with a newer minute and the new median, minting two immutable Tickers that carry the same 12 bytes. Consumers spend a Ticker in their own transaction; the covenant on the Ticker makes them recreate it at the same index, which chains their transaction to the update that minted it.

The only parties with keys are the thirteen notaries. Each is a TLSNotary verifier pinned to one exchange endpoint. It co-runs (MPC-TLS) or forwards (proxy mode) the prover's TLS session, validates the exchange's certificate chain against public roots, requires the entire request and response to be revealed, checks the request against its pin, parses the price, and signs a statement for that Unix minute. It signs at most one statement per minute and holds no funds.

Provers (anyone) open the sessions. Builders (anyone) collect statements and post updates; if two builders post the same minute, the network's first-seen rule picks one. The head carries a BCH reserve and pays its own fees, so building needs no coins. Sweepers (anyone) reclaim Ticker dust after a day.

§02Trust model

What consensus enforces

  • At least 7 distinct table rows signed the same (category, row, exchange, minute, price); each signature is checked with OP_CHECKDATASIG against the pubkey compiled into the covenant.
  • The value written is the lower median of those prices.
  • The minute strictly increases. There is no timestamp, no block height, no timelock anywhere in the covenant.
  • The head, its category and minting capability survive; exactly two Tickers of 708 sats are minted with the same commitment; the reserve drops by at most 5,000 sats per update.

Who must collude to forge the price

Seven of thirteen notaries. That is the same threshold the previous design had for its publishers, but the parties are different: notaries never choose a number, they verify a TLS session with a named exchange and sign what it said. A single notary that lies is one outlier in a median; six liars still leave the result inside the honest range. The design assumes the thirteen are operated independently; while ticker.cash runs them all, the honest description is "one operator who cryptographically verified each exchange".

What nobody can do

  • Post a statement for another deployment, minute, row or exchange: all five are in the signed message.
  • Count a notary twice: rows must be strictly ascending in an update.
  • Drain the reserve: spending it requires a valid quorum for a new minute, and honest notaries sign one minute per minute.
  • Forge Tickers: the category's only minting NFT is the head, and the covenant pins every output.
Time. A Bitcoin-family covenant can prove "not before" but never "not after", so no oracle can prove freshness on chain. v25 does not pretend to: the minute is attested by the signing notaries, consensus enforces only order, and consumers judge freshness from the minute in the commitment.

§03One minute

  1. At second two of minute m, a prover connects to each row's notary, announces {"v":25,"row":r,"seq":m}, and opens the TLSNotary session: GET <pinned path> to the pinned exchange host.
  2. The notary validates the certificate chain and the handshake, receives the fully revealed transcript, checks method, path, Host and status 200, extracts the price from the JSON field it is pinned to, and signs. The statement is served at GET /v1/statement?seq=m.
  3. A builder fetches /v1/latest from every notary, groups statements by minute, and when a minute newer than the head has at least seven, builds and broadcasts one transaction.
  4. On one-minute blocks the update lands in the next block. When a block is slow, the next minute's update spends the unconfirmed head; a builder may also skip minutes and post only the newest.
  5. Consumers spend Tickers; after 144 legacy relative-lock units (one day before and after Faster Blocks) anyone sweeps them.

§04The statement

message   = 0x4e ‖ category(32, VM byte order) ‖ row(1) ‖ source(1) ‖ seq(4, u32 LE) ‖ price(8, u64 LE)   (47 B)
signature = Schnorr(secp256k1) over sha256(message)      (64 B; what OP_CHECKDATASIG verifies)
price     = USD per BCH in 1e-8 units
seq       = floor(unix_time / 60)

The covenant rebuilds the message from its own token category (reversed display order), the row byte supplied in the update, the source byte stored in that row's table entry, the sequence and the supplied price. Only the price and the signature travel in the transaction.

The notary table compiled into the covenant is 13 × (pubkey 33 ‖ source 1) = 442 bytes; the deployment manifest publishes it row by row together with each notary's operator and endpoints.

The fee is exact because the transaction is fixed-width before it is built: the Oracle input carries no transaction signature and Schnorr signatures never vary in size. At one update a minute that is about 0.026 to 0.032 BCH a day, sweeps included. The reserve also parks 2 × 708 sats per update in Tickers, which sweepers reclaim.

§06The covenant

Oracle.cash (373 bytes, cashc 0.14, P2SH-32) has one function, update(seq, rows, prices, sigs, median):

  1. seq.length == 4, int(seq) > int(commit[0..4)).
  2. k ≥ 7, prices.length == 8k, sigs.length == 64k, median.length == 8.
  3. For each row: rows strictly ascending; entry = table[row]; checkDataSig(sig, 0x4e ‖ cat ‖ row ‖ source ‖ seq ‖ price, pubkey); count prices below and above the claimed median in the same pass.
  4. The median was found, with at most (k−1)/2 prices below and k/2 above: the unique lower median.
  5. Exactly three outputs. Output 0 is the head: same locking bytecode, same category with minting capability, commitment seq ‖ median, no fungible amount.
  6. Outputs 1 and 2 are Tickers at the pinned P2S lock, same category, immutable, same commitment, exactly 708 sats.
  7. value(head′) ≥ value(head) − 5,000.

Ticker.cash (31 bytes, P2S): consume() recreates the Ticker at the same index with the same lock, category and value; sweep() requires this.age ≥ 144 and nothing else.

Source, compiled artifacts and the VM test suite are in contracts/ of the repository; the suite drives a forged transaction into every require.

§07Integrate

On chain

Spend a Ticker as an input of your transaction and recreate it at the same output index (Ticker.consume enforces lock, category and value). Pin the category and the Ticker locking bytecode from the manifest, then read the commitment:

require(tx.inputs[1].lockingBytecode == tickerLock);          // 31-byte P2S lock from manifest.json
require(tx.inputs[1].tokenCategory == oracleCategoryVm);     // 32 bytes, reversed display id (immutable: no capability byte)
bytes c  = tx.inputs[1].nftCommitment;                         // 12 bytes
int seq   = int(c.slice(0, 4));                               // Unix minute of observation
int price = int(c.slice(4, 12));                              // USD per BCH × 1e8
require(seq >= minSeq); require(seq <= maxSeq);              // your freshness floor and ceiling

The reference consumer contracts/PriceGated.cash (41 bytes) does exactly this. Keep a maxSeq ceiling: without one, a counterparty can hold an old Ticker chain and surface it later.

Off chain

Everything a client needs is in /manifest.json: category, head and Ticker locking bytecode, the notary table and an Electrum pool. The head's current commitment is the minting NFT of the category at the head address; blockchain.scripthash.listunspent with include_tokens returns it. This page's stats view is built on nothing else.

§08Run a notary

A notary is one small Rust binary, one exchange endpoint, one session a minute. It holds no funds and needs no exchange account. Bandwidth per session is about 1.5 MB in proxy mode and about 43 MB in MPC mode; both finish in under a second against every pinned endpoint.

ticker-notary --home ~/.ticker-notary-7 init --row 7 --source 8 \
    --category <deployment category> --listen 0.0.0.0:7007 --api 0.0.0.0:7107 \
    --mode both --operator "your name"
ticker-notary --home ~/.ticker-notary-7 run          # systemd unit in node/systemd/

Rows are assigned at genesis: the table is compiled into the covenant, so joining means being in the table of the next deployment. Until then the useful thing to do is run the binary against the current category with your own key, publish your /v1/info, and show a month of statements that match the chain.

Exchanges

The source byte in every statement names an entry of an append-only registry (node/core/src/sources.rs): host, path and the JSON field the notary signs. An id is never redefined; replacing an exchange means a new id and a new table. Ids 4 (Binance.US), 8 (EXMO) and 9 (Independent Reserve) were retired on 2026-10-24 when their BCH/USD markets went quiet. Six rows quote USD, three USDC, four USDT, so a single stablecoin losing its peg leaves at least seven unaffected rows.

§10FAQ

Why can't the chain check that the minute is fresh?

Because a valid transaction must stay valid forever; consensus can bound time from below (locktimes) but never from above. The minute is signed by seven or more notaries and consumers compare it to their own clock. After the Faster Blocks upgrade a legacy one-block relative lock means ten one-minute blocks, which is one more reason the cadence lives in signatures rather than timelocks.

Why not every block?

On one-minute blocks this is every block. Minutes are better than heights because notaries agree on NTP time within a second, while tip views diverge around every block, and because a slow block still gets a fresh price each minute.

What if a notary is down?

Its row is missing that minute; seven of thirteen suffice. A permanent loss is handled at the next genesis, since the table is compiled into the covenant.

Who pays?

The head. Each update may spend at most 5,000 sats of its reserve: the exact fee plus two 708-sat Tickers. Anyone can top the reserve up by adding a coin to an update. Sweeping Tickers returns most of the dust to whoever sweeps.

Is this real money?

The live deployment is on chipnet, Bitcoin Cash's six-months-early test network. Mainnet follows once independent notaries hold rows.
